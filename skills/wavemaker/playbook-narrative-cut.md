## Narrative short or trailer: cut, finish, review and deliver

Part of [playbook-narrative.md](playbook-narrative.md).

**Radio edit, then picture.** For: a cut timed to the performance, so no line crosses a cut mid-phrase. Lock the read before cutting the picture, and the score too when it leads (a cue you will keep as it is); a score written to the picture comes after a draft cut instead (SKILL.md default 9). Lay the chosen lines, and any locked cue, end to end, tighten accidental hesitations on the word times (keep the beats the script wrote), never speed a read up. Then cut picture to it: each line over the shot it belongs to, cuts in the gaps between lines or on a musical hit, shots without lines absorbing the length; draft to the runtime with beats, never slowed takes or held stills. A shot shown twice reads as filler: a montage short of shots gets more takes, not repeats (DUST RUN's climax showed the ship igniting and the truck from behind twice each within 7 s). Measure the score's level over time before cutting to it (`listen` `level_dbfs_per_s`): a music plan's sections are requests, and one run's "epic climax" section came back 4 dB quieter than its chase, which sat flat at one level for 18 s. Directed reads ran 20-50% longer, and dropped into the unchanged picture 7-9 of 10 cyberpunk-trailer lines crossed a cut mid-phrase. `compose`'s `cut_inside_speech` gives `cut_to_s` for each cut inside a phrase and `long_pause_inside_line` the `trim_s` for each unwritten pause; a cut in a pause with the line carrying over (an L-cut) is fine. Worked example: the cyberpunk trailer's re-cut in [voice-performance-direction](wm://card/voice-performance-direction). A trailer is also cut for contrast ([trailer-cut-rhythm](wm://card/trailer-cut-rhythm)): the 18 horror trailers we measured accelerated into a montage, put short cards between images, dropped to near-silence before their biggest hits and landed the title at 85-96%; two or three windows from each take give the cut the shots for that. Other genres and lengths cut differently: measure a reference for the brief. The mix keeps that shape: the climax before the title is the loudest stretch of the cut, and `listen`'s `level_dbfs_per_s` on the final shows whether it is (DUST RUN's climactic shout sat at about -25 LUFS short-term, 5 dB under its opening launch). The horror trailer used each of its 16 takes once, at a steady 2.6 s a shot, and put a 5.5 s title at 80%.

**Finish.** For: one film at the delivery size, with one look over every shot. Every kept take is at the delivery size: native 1088p (see Takes), or, for a take that profile cannot make (an FL2VA landing, over 124 frames), super-resolved: FlashVSR when a face is in frame (`edit` `upscale`, `params: {model: "flashvsr", scale: 2}`), SeedVR2 otherwise (`params: {model: "seedvr2", target_short_side: 1080}`; on faces it reads as plastic). Re-run a take at 20 steps (`h3-fl-native`) first where lettering or fine detail has to hold. Then `compose` the final once at the delivery size: the destination's, else 1920x1080 for 16:9, 1080x1920 for 9:16, 1080x1080 for 1:1. All three trailers delivered 1344x768 material, and a 2:00 short delivered 1344x768 takes scaled 1.43x by the compose with an hour of its turn left. Then finish the composed film as one: `style.look`, per-clip `colorGains`, a vignette `overlay` and a grade (`style.filmGrade`, `style.colorGrade`) in the timeline, then `edit` `camera` `{motion: "none", look: "film", grain_pct, vignette_stops}` on the composed file, or `edit` `grade` there if the timeline did not grade (under 0.2 credits a minute), and look at the darkest scenes, the cards and the title afterwards ([finish-pass](wm://card/finish-pass)). The title is designed art: a full-frame title image (TitleCard `backgroundImageAssetKey`; `titleTreatmentAssetKey` draws it small), or an H3 take of it that does something.

**Review and deliver.** For: a final measured against the brief before it goes out. `review` the exact final, with the brief's must-haves as `requirements` (each beat with its planned time, every quoted card, label and end-card line, each spoken line) and `characters` carrying each lead's `plate` so the identity section measures every shot's faces against them (the 80s re-run listed its sections and passed no plates, so nothing measured its faces), and `expected_text` naming each shot's in-scene words with its times, so the text section reads them back. A `defect` on a speaking take means a retake or a cutaway (the cartoon-adaptation trailer shipped two). Collect `pending` sections before delivering unless the turn is near its limit, and then say what was not reviewed ([pitfalls-delivery.md](pitfalls-delivery.md)).
