## Narrative short or trailer

A narrated or scripted short of up to about 90 s (a sketch, a mock documentary, a joke) has a shorter path that starts from the words and the voice: [story-short-method](wm://card/story-short-method).

**What usually works.** Hook, want, complication (ideally caused by the hero's flaw), a turn around two thirds in, an ending that pays off a plant. Trailers: cold open, world, inciting incident, escalation, title, button. Shorts 60-300 s, trailers 45-120 s; shots 2-6 s; trailers usually speed up toward a late title, horror most of all (2-4 s in the opening to 0.2-0.8 s in the montage); what each genre and length measured is in [reference-rhythm-by-genre](wm://card/reference-rhythm-by-genre), and how to cut one in [trailer-cut-rhythm](wm://card/trailer-cut-rhythm). A genre sets the look and the sound as much as the story: horror is mostly dark frames lit by one source in the scene, near-silence and hits ([genre-horror](wm://card/genre-horror)); thriller, action, comedy and drama have cards too. Characters speak on camera in speaking takes: one speaker per take, cut on the reply, listeners' lips closed, one locked voice per character. Few characters and places keep identity manageable: plate each character first and build every keyframe from the plates. Each scene benefits from a clear axis and an establishing view. With two or more people in one space, writing down who sits where in frame terms, making the scene's master keyframe first and building each single and over-the-shoulder as a restage of it keeps seats, backgrounds and eyelines consistent; keyframes made in parallel from the plates each invent their own ([blocking-before-coverage](wm://card/blocking-before-coverage), examples in [reference-scene-blocking-examples](wm://card/reference-scene-blocking-examples)). The master shows one side of the room; an empty view of each wall the coverage faces, edited from the plate, keeps reverse angles in the same room ([location-sheet](wm://card/location-sheet)). Sitcom and animated comedy: even 30 s is a dialogue scene with setup, escalation and a button, the sound of every physical gag (none added to a line or a look) and room tone underneath. Lines written for the voice model's read, speaking shots cut on or near the last word (or carried onto a reaction), reaction shots of the listener and the punch word last tend to make dialogue land; a face held after its line reads as dubbed ([dialogue-scene-craft](wm://card/dialogue-scene-craft)). A film of singles reads as a slideshow and hides its physical beats; an FL2VA shot between before and after stills, an insert, or a reaction with its sound puts a slap or a hand-over on screen, and non-speaking two-shots and keyframes restaged from the master vary the coverage ([narrative-coverage-and-interaction](wm://card/narrative-coverage-and-interaction)). A premise comedy (a horror film where everyone is sensible) is a run of one joke: each beat stages the genre's cliché so the viewer expects the mistake (the sting, the cursed book, the basement door), then the sensible choice, then a reaction or button; the beats escalate, and a runner (the killer's growing frustration) builds across them. The same holds when an interface is the joke (life sold as a subscription): each gag is setup, gag, reaction and a beat, with the sound of what's on screen and no novelty effect, cards held long enough to read, physical gags on camera, and the brief's contrasts and last image kept ([premise-comedy-beats](wm://card/premise-comedy-beats)). A genre parody (a telenovela, a noir) plays the genre's form straight; its held title cards are image-model stills, its supers over motion stay in `compose` ([genre-parody](wm://card/genre-parody)).

**Graphs and pathways.** `pathway:grounded-trailer`, `pathway:production`, `pathway:pre-production`, `pathway:world` (series bible, cast, voices and look across episodes).

**What has worked**, stage by stage. Each stage says what it is for and what went wrong without it. The order is the one that has worked, not a rule: change it when the piece asks. A piece with no dialogue has no voice stage; a score that leads is locked before the cut. Voice comes before any take that speaks, because a speaking take is generated with its rendered line as the guide.

**Pitfalls**: continuity at cuts and coverage without geography ([pitfalls-identity.md](pitfalls-identity.md)); lip sync, muted takes that keep talking, lines longer than the take ([pitfalls-speech.md](pitfalls-speech.md)); flat character reads and voice drift ([pitfalls-narration.md](pitfalls-narration.md)).

**Questions to ask of the cut.**
- With the sound off, does it look like its genre and like one film: light from sources in the scene, the share of darkness the look bible set, subjects placed rather than centred by default, one finish over every shot? With the picture off, does it sound like one: quiet that the hits land against, a signature sound, dialogue at one level?
- Does the cut's rhythm suit its genre and length: how it builds, its quiet before the big moments, where the title lands and what follows it? `review`'s `craft` section has the numbers, beside a film you name or [the measured ranges](wm://card/reference-rhythm-by-genre).
- Does the opening hook, the turn land and the ending pay off the plant?
- Is each beat covered from several angles of the same moment rather than one medium shot, with a line only ever over the mouth that speaks it or over no mouth?
- Could a viewer draw the room and follow geography across every cut? Does each person keep their side of frame, eyeline and background within a scene?
- Is every scripted line present, in sync and in its locked voice, and does each read play its moment?
- Does each line play over its own shot, with cuts between lines or on a hit and no accidental pause inside a line (`compose`'s `cut_inside_speech`, `long_pause_inside_line`)?
- Does the cut move without melting faces: each shot's action reads, faces hold mid-motion at full size, no take slowed below 1.0x?
- Is it the same face for each lead in every shot, measured against the plate?
- Is it the same room, props and vehicles in every shot of a scene (`compare` `setting` on keyframes, `review` `looking` continuity on the cut)?
- Is the runtime within about 3 s of the brief, and is the delivery resolution what was asked, from native 1088p or super-resolved takes (FlashVSR on faces) rather than a plain scale?
- Were the defects the review and delivery checks found fixed before the last `deliver`? Does the final message say what was made, with a defect still unfixed in one plain sentence rather than a list, and state every disclosure plainly: upscales, procedural audio and review sections not collected?
