## Narrative short or trailer: script, look, plates and keyframes

Part of [playbook-narrative.md](playbook-narrative.md). Each stage: what it is for, then what went wrong without it.

**Script and critique.** For: a story that fits its runtime and pays off what it sets up, settled while changes cost a message rather than a render. A beat sheet timed to the stated runtime within about 3 s, a reaction for every gag, and the plants with their payoffs listed; then a critique pass before any paid picture ([script-critique-before-production](wm://card/script-critique-before-production)). The horror comedy planned 109.5 s for a 2:00 brief. A script the user supplies gets the same read: keep their words when they ask, and name its problems and fixes in the plan before spending. In a branching or poll piece, each choice should cause its ending, and each setup should be answered in every branch (an interactive film's "Look up" led to doors opening on space, and the roof knocks paid off only in one ending). A card says what no line says: DUST RUN's "THE LAST SHIP LEAVES AT SUNDOWN" followed Mara saying the same thing. People talk in contractions ("We're getting on it", not "We are going to be on it"). Give the leads a want and a cost the viewer can name, and open on a hook in the first five seconds: one run spent its first 25 s on two lines and a drone at -32 LUFS.

**Look.** For: one film's light and framing, decided before any picture is made. What worked on the horror trailer: before any plate or keyframe, a short look bible in `plan.md`, per world or act: the light sources in the scene and where they sit, the light's direction and how dark the shadow side is, how much of the frame is near-black, two or three colours and the accent that breaks them, lens and camera height for each kind of shot, where people sit in the frame and what the empty space holds, and the texture the finish will add ([look-and-light-before-keyframes](wm://card/look-and-light-before-keyframes); horror: [genre-horror](wm://card/genre-horror)). Keyframe prompts that start with the scene's light and position in frame, described physically, changed the picture; quality words ("cinematic", "horror film still", "film grain") changed nothing. The horror trailer prompted "overcast flat polar light" and a desk lamp lighting her face "warmly from the front", centred 14 of 16 subjects and came out evenly lit; the same references with a look bible put two thirds of each interior frame in shadow and the subjects off centre. Looking at each still against the bible before animating catches the misses cheaply (Gemini ignored a requested high angle and a 200mm lens).

**Plates.** For: the same people, places and props in every shot. A place is written as a set before it is pictured (size, where each camera stands, fixtures, no mirrors), and its empty plates are made from one another and checked as a pair ([location-plate](wm://card/location-plate); the elevator-scene plates mixed outside and inside views). One per character, place and recurring prop or vehicle (the horror comedy's car changed model; [object-and-known-thing-plate](wm://card/object-and-known-thing-plate)). A real person or a known character's plate is an edit of a picture of them (SKILL default 5): two runs of an 80s-characters film drew every plate from "played by…" text and the faces read as lookalikes.

**Hero keyframes.** For: each lead's face approved once, so every other shot of them is an edit of it. One approved hero keyframe per lead, looked at full size (`wm get`, then Read it), not on a contact sheet; one keyframe per shot, re-made only when that check fails it. That person's other shots are edits of it (Grok edit kept a plate's identity best, card `grok-imagine-image`), checked with `compare` `aspect: "identity"` against the plate before animating. The cartoon-adaptation trailer's lead was three different men ([pitfalls-identity.md](pitfalls-identity.md)). Within a scene, a change of light or state and every insert are edits of an approved keyframe rather than new pictures, and `compare` `aspect: "setting"` against the location plate or master catches a changed room before it is animated (an interactive film's cab changed ceiling and doors in five shots).

**Geography.** For: who sits where, which way things travel and when, the same in every shot. In `plan.md`, before keyframes: each vehicle's wheel side and who sits in each seat, the way it travels, where the threat and the goal lie, and the time of day per beat in story order. Each still prompt then turns those into frame sides from where its camera stands: facing a vehicle or its occupants from the front swaps left and right (a left-hand-drive driver is on frame right through the windshield); a camera behind a traveller has the goal ahead and the threat behind the lens, out of shot; a cab seen from outside names who is at the wheel, or the model seats a stranger. Then read each still back from the picture, not the prompt: which way the vehicle points, whose hands are on the wheel and on which side of the vehicle, where the threat is against the travel, whether the light is later than the shot before. DUST RUN planned Mara driving on the left but wrote "Mara in the left half of the frame" for cameras through the windshield, so four cab shots put her on the right, while the exterior launch showed someone else at a left-hand wheel; its ramp still, approved as "heads for the ramp", showed the truck driving past the ship into the storm; and a daylight canyon jump came after the dusk scenes. With this paragraph its next run got every seat and the storm right, but not the light: race and cab stills prompted "golden hour" and "at dusk" came back in white midday light between dusk shots and passed. Write the light as what the camera sees (the sun's height and side, the sky's colour, shadow length: "the sun touching the horizon, an orange sky, shadows longer than the truck"), and read each still's light against its neighbours in story order, not in the order they were made; one that is earlier gets an edit of its light or a remake.


**Stills as one film.** For: one world that a viewer believes, before any take is paid for. Every place a scene returns to gets a plate (the settlement, the ship, the ground they cross), and its shots are edits of it, as with the cast. Then lay every keyframe out in cut order and look at them together, on mute, as the film: the same ground, buildings, vehicle parts and sizes in every shot; a reverse angle in the same place and light as its master; nothing that reads as a 3D render or a cut-out pasted on a background (clean plastic shapes, glow with no source, a vehicle with no contact with the ground under it). `review` with `stills` checks one scene's keyframes in cut order. Light is checked the same way, shot by shot: in `plan.md`, beside each shot's planned time of day, write the light as read from its still (the sun's height and side, the sky's colour, how long the shadows are), and remake any still whose light reads earlier than the shot before it or than its beat. The fourth run's prompts said "sunset", and its last line still played in daylight after the sun had set. DUST RUN's fourth run, watched on mute: "unnatural and fake looking and things dont fit together properly". Its ground went from dunes to salt to canyon, the ship was three rockets, and the robot changed size; none of four runs called `review` with `stills`.
