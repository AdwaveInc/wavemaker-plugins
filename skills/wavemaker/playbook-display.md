## Display ad set

**What usually works.** A banner is seen for a second at its real pixels: one message, the brand, one action. The site gives real offerings, wording, phone and hours; the logo gives palette hex and lettering style. Most viewers have never heard of the brand, so the words tell a stranger what this is and what they get ([copy-a-stranger-understands](wm://card/copy-a-stranger-understands)). A short brief keeps the set coherent: what it is, who it's for and the moment they're in, the facts, the argument, the look, a line for each size, the CTA as what they get, disclosures. When the ask is only a link or a sentence, that thinking is yours to do ([ad-brief-from-a-thin-ask](wm://card/ad-brief-from-a-thin-ask)). The unit is usually a design rather than a staged scene: the same colours, type and logo on every size, in a look chosen for the words ([designed-ad-looks](wm://card/designed-ad-looks)), with a picture only when it earns its place ([key-visual-proves-the-offer](wm://card/key-visual-proves-the-offer)). Supplied photos of the business beat generated ones. The product a unit shows is the real one (hard rule 8): the named model's photos on every render that shows it, its stated size in one sentence anchored to a person at the same depth, and `review` with `products` on each unit that shows it. Each size is its own layout: 728x90, 970x250, 320x50 and 320x100 carry logo, one line and CTA; towers stack picture, line and CTA. The image model makes every unit whole at its own shape, type and logo included; nothing is laid out in code. Gemini 3 Pro letters the 300x250, 336x280 and squares; Seedream 5.0 letters strips and towers at their exact pixels, where Gemini Flash garbles, with no GPU start. Design the 300x250 first, then make every other size at once, two takes each, from the same scene, palette, type description and locked lines, so the set reads as one campaign: about 30 s to the first unit and 2 minutes to the set. The same words don't give the same person, so a set with a person decides how to hold them ([display-iab-set](wm://card/display-iab-set)). Every word and the logo at least 6% in from each edge (`review` `kind: "display"` measures it); smallest text at least 11 px at 300x250. Category notes: political sets carry the supplied "Paid for by" line on every size; legal, medical and financial copy avoids guarantees; food reads best as the real dish; apps show a supplied screenshot rather than a drawn interface, or the result in the customer's world ([software-and-apps](wm://card/software-and-apps)).

**Graphs and pathways.** `pathway:display-ads`, `pathway:directed-stills`, `pathway:ad-images`, `pathway:native-format-stills`. Locked lines written first; `search` `kinds: ["format"]` for the sizes and `read wm://format/<id>` for each unit's `lettering_routes` (the `generate` call at its shape); the calls are in [examples.md](examples.md) §8. The supplied logo goes in as the first reference on Gemini; on a Seedream unit it is a second Seedream edit with the unit and the logo as references (examples.md §8). `edit` `ads_format` with the unit's `format` and `fit: "cover"` takes each render to its exact pixels, cropping no more than the route's `cover_crop`, as a JPEG under the unit's file cap. Review both takes of each size as they land (`review` `kind: "display"` with `expected` lines; it measures words and placement, not the ad) and keep the better of those that pass, looking at both at full size, because review measures words and placement, not the picture (when speed matters more, the first that passes). When the words are yours, take a cold read of the kept unit before delivering it ([review-stranger-read](wm://card/review-stranger-read)), and look at the kept units together at real size. `deliver` it then (`delivery_set`, the set's `deliverable_id`): the caller shows each piece at once and the outcome lists them all. Fix what is really wrong and re-review only those sizes. When the set is HTML5, write each size's index.html (head `<meta name="ad.size" content="width=W,height=H">`, local images, CSS or JS motion that stops) and `compose {html, output: {format: "html5", destination: "google/display-html5"}}` per size; watch the preview, then deliver the ZIPs and backups together as `delivery_set` to the same destination.

**Pitfalls.** A line a stranger can't decode, or a trial of who knows what; a person alone with a device as the whole idea; a dark frame that turns to mud at real size; a headline squeezed whole into a strip or tower; a logo pass fed a crop of an earlier render; the master scaled or cropped into other sizes; type, logo or layout drawn in code; a strip or tower lettered by Gemini Flash; a painted logo instead of the supplied file; a model writing its own copy; invented prices, reviews or phone numbers; a product drawn from words or another model's photos, or several times its size; an edit that keeps an invented product (review the source with `products` first and carry the photos into the edit).

**Questions to ask of the cut** (each unit at real pixels, e.g. `wm get <sha256>` and Read it full size).
- Could a stranger say who it's from, what it is and what they get? One message, readable in a second? Could a competitor run it unchanged?
- Side by side, is it one campaign? Is the largest type on each unit the message? Is anything in the picture impossible?
- Every word correct, true and legible? Logo faithful, palette only? An image model redraws a logo, so "the real logo" is true only of the file: say it was added by the model and compared.
- Every requested size present at exact pixels and under its file cap?
- Is every fact traceable to what was sent or found?
- Is the product the named model, from its photos, at its stated size? Where locked copy contradicts the product page, is it kept as sent and the difference said?
