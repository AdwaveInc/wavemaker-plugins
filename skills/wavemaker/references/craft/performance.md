## Performance

### Use when

Directing or judging visible acting, movement, delivery and synchronized speech.

### Techniques and tradeoffs

Give action and speech an intention, a relationship and a state change. Gesture, gaze, timing and restraint can communicate more than exaggerated movement. A performance may be still or energetic; judge what the brief asks. Preserve the requested character and manner without stripping personality to simplify generation. Conditioning text/audio and references can support a performance but do not establish likeness, exact speech or synchronization. Measure the actual guide’s offsets and listen/view the result. A voice line is a performance too: give it the scene, what the character wants, the emotion and intensity and the word it turns on, written where the speech model reads it (tags, punctuation), and render a scene's lines together. A flat read, one intensity throughout or the wrong emotion for the moment is a defect, as much as over-acting. Multiple speakers and wide framing are choices to assess, not automatic failures.

### Questions to inspect

Does the action or line communicate the intended purpose? Are gaze, body, voice and timing coherent? Does each read play its moment, with the stress on the word the line turns on? Is the requested recognizable subject maintained? Are exact speech and lip sync actually supported by playback? Does the emotion survive cuts? Is a reported defect visible or audible in the reviewed scope?
