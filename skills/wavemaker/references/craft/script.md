## Script

### Use when

Developing or revising authored words and dramatic structure.

### Techniques and tradeoffs

Actions, choices and consequences can carry meaning more economically than explanation. Setups make later changes understandable; withheld information can create curiosity if the audience still has enough orientation. A sequence may be dramatic, observational, graphic or argumentative. Choose structure for the brief rather than a universal hook/turn/button formula. Let useful images carry information instead of narrating everything shown. Preserve exact locked words and source facts; distinguish supplied requirements from the draft’s own inventions. A draft can remain concise without flattening personality or atmosphere.

### Questions to inspect

What changes or becomes clearer? Are important events prepared? Can the audience follow cause and consequence? Do words add something to the picture? Are continuity and factual claims sound? Does a revision preserve the requested idea and exact locks?
