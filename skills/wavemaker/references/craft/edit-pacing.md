## Edit and pacing

### Use when

Arranging takes, graphics, speech and transitions into a finished piece.

### Techniques and tradeoffs

Cut on information, action, reaction, rhythm or a deliberate contrast. Hold when understanding or emotion benefits; cut when a new view earns its place. Professional medians give a feel for pace: social shorts around 1-2.5 s, ads 1-3 s, trailers 1.5-4 s, narrative 2-6 s, documentary B-roll 4-8 s; a spoken line sets its own length, and one length throughout reads mechanical. A generated 10 s take is raw material: its best 1-3 s often make the shot. Continuous narration or dialogue can span many visual cuts. J/L cuts let sound lead or outlast a picture cut; a sound bridge can connect otherwise discontinuous scenes. Music videos usually cut on bars and change energy at section boundaries, so cuts feel musical; break that on purpose. A long hold on a still or a repeated take reads as padding unless it is a deliberate beat. Inspect the whole cut for accumulated repetition, unintended dead time, missing payoff and abrupt joins. Trim from actual selected sources rather than invented capacity.

### Questions to inspect

What earns each cut or hold? Does the audience have enough time to understand? Are pauses intentional and speech complete? Does the ending land? Does every requested event survive the edit? Are transition overlaps and source trims coherent? Does playback support the pacing judgment?
