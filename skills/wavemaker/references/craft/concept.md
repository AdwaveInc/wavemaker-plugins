## Audience, proposition and argument

### Use when

Choosing the idea for an ad, story, presentation or explainer.

### Techniques and tradeoffs

Identify what the audience already knows, wants or doubts. A concrete proposition, relevant proof and useful action can organize an ad; they need not appear as a fixed sequence. Show a benefit through consequence or demonstration when that is stronger than a claim. In fiction, desire, resistance and change can give an image sequence purpose. Originality should sharpen the requested idea, not replace it. Source-bound claims need supplied evidence; fiction may invent within the brief. A concept that only lists features reads as a brochure; one idea the viewer can retell is stronger.

### Questions to inspect

What is the audience meant to notice, feel or understand? Why this subject or product? Does the strongest moment support the proposition? Are factual claims sourced? Does the ending deliver the intended action or emotional consequence?
