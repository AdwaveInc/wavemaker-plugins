## Narrative short or trailer: voice and takes

Part of [playbook-narrative.md](playbook-narrative.md). Voice comes before the takes that speak: a speaking take is generated with its rendered line as the guide (defaults 8).

**Voice.** For: lines that play their moment, rendered before the takes that speak them and the cut that is timed to them. Characters cast as people (a plain brief of their ordinary voice; the audition whose pitch and sound fit them), range tested with a rendered peak line, and each line directed for its scene before it is rendered: what the character wants, the emotion and intensity, the word to hit, turned into one short tag a sentence at most and punctuation. Character lines on `eleven_v4` at stability about 0.35 with the lines around them as `previous_text`/`next_text`, each voice checked against its character and one model per character; narration as one continuous read; cut on word times, with each shot as long as its read, never the read squeezed into a shot already cut; no line under about 2 s rendered on its own. Key lines taken three times and chosen against the note (passed as `delivery` to `listen` and `review`; `listen`'s `prosody` shows a flat or rushed read), heard under the score; intensity builds to the drop before the title. `listen` with `expected_text`; pieces levelled to one loudness; act on the listener's casting notes. Write the note beside each line in `plan.md` (to whom, what they want, intensity 1-5, the hit word) before rendering; three runs that skipped it sent three-adjective tags. Quiet lines are low, close and voiced: Theo's quiet line came back whispered with no pitch in all three runs ("barely above a whisper", "[quiet, scared]", "[quiet, trembling, stubborn]" on `eleven_v4`), and a whisper loses whose voice it is. A shout is raised from the chest, not pitched up: Mara's shouted lines rose 12-17 semitones over her speaking pitch, to over 400 Hz, and in one run the transcriber heard a different speaker. Check each line by its numbers before it is cut: `listen` on the line measures its median pitch in hertz (its prosody); take each character's plain speaking lines as their pitch, and retake a raised line more than about 7 semitones above it (12 x log2 of the ratio; 1.5 x the hertz), or a quiet line whose pitch comes back null or `band_limited` (a whisper). DUST RUN's fourth run, which had notes for every line, still sent Mara's command at 315 Hz, 12 semitones over her 160 Hz. A flat read is a defect ([voice-performance-direction](wm://card/voice-performance-direction), [pitfalls-narration.md](pitfalls-narration.md)).

**Takes.** For: motion that reads, from keyframes that already hold the look. The still already holds the look; the take prompt says what moves and how the light changes, plus one camera behaviour chosen for the beat (locked off for dread, a slow creep for someone watching, handheld only for panic or a point of view). On the horror trailer's frozen face, "the camera pushes in slowly; his face stays centred in frame" made a bright, live portrait, while "the beam drifts right across the ice; as it passes his eye the eye flares pale, then the face sinks back into shadow; camera locked off" kept it dark and dead. Name what must stay in frame only when it could leave; don't pin a subject to the centre. The keyframe is the instant before the action: a look-up cannot play when the keyframe already looks. One clear action per take at a natural pace, written in frame terms; shots that hold faces stay gentle, and fast body action goes on wide shots (stacked fast actions smeared faces on turbo H3); takes prompted "slowly" barely moved (one trailer's seven measured 1.6-7.6 against 23-30 for its two fast ones). Speaking takes: a small beat under the line or a camera behaviour chosen for the moment, never "keeps still while he speaks"; the still carries the eyes and the face the line needs, since the take rarely changes either: eyes on the person the line is said to, and the expression named in the prompt, because image models smile unless told otherwise (DUST RUN's alarm lines "It's right behind us!", "Everything you've got!" and "Hold it together!" were all played grinning across three runs, and "We always make it" smiled into the lens instead of at her brother). An exchange is played between the two people, with both in frame or eyelines that meet across the cut: one run played its emotional exchange as two singles each looking out of a different window (an elevator scene's dialogue shots left it 0.85 near-static), and not the same move on every shot. Look at each face mid-motion at full size before keeping a take. Takes for a 1080p film render natively at 1920x1088 (profiles and frame limits: defaults 1; a speaking take: defaults 8). One take per shot first, each at least 1.5x the window you will cut; a second take only for a shot whose take failed `watch` or `review`, that shot alone, never the whole batch (`count: 2` only with a stated reason, such as fast travel, body actions or speaking takes; a 2:00 short kept the first of two takes in 26 of 28 shots); cut the moving middle; never `playbackRate` below 1.0 to fill time. A travel shot that has to arrive pins its last frame (FL2VA). Words that must read (a can, a sign, a screen) go on the reviewed keyframe, held from the first frame, or in a `compose` layer; motion garbles type it has to carry or invents (a four-letter brand name misspelled in the launch films).
