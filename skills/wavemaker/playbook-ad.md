## Ad or TV spot

**What usually works.** A hook in the first couple of seconds, the problem, the product in use as visible proof, the result, then brand and call to action; satire runs premise, escalations and a button. 15-30 s (presenter spots 20-40 s). The brand seen or heard early; the CTA last and on screen about 2 s. The picture is generated footage; cards and graphics support it. Product ads usually carry a voiceover (about 2.4 words per second; a 30 s spot carries 60-70 words); presenters speak on camera. Shots around 1-3 s, held longer when the brief is calm or cinematic; a presenter talking to camera is one performance with the cuts over it ([continuous-monologue](wm://card/continuous-monologue)). A sound on every visible action, and none added to a spoken joke. An ad about a real person tends to show them: reviewed photos, moved and graded, and facts from cited sources. Political spots carry the brief's sponsor line.

**Graphs and pathways.** `pathway:presenter-ad`, `pathway:product-campaign`, `pathway:grounded-product-promo`, `pathway:ad-recipes` (25 reference-conditioned recipes), `pathway:saved-voice-h3` for presenters. A product spin or a product moving in its scene: [graph-product-video](wm://card/graph-product-video). The same ad in another language: [recipe-dub-ad-into-another-language](wm://card/recipe-dub-ad-into-another-language). Concepts and every word written before any paid picture ([ad-brief-from-a-thin-ask](wm://card/ad-brief-from-a-thin-ask) when the ask is thin); `review` for the set.

**What a good spot gets right.** These are goals, not steps: reach each one the way the piece allows, and skip what it does not need. Worked detail and the measured runs are in [reference-ad-playbook-evidence](wm://card/reference-ad-playbook-evidence).

- **Whose ad it is, early and clearly.** Viewers settle whose ad they are watching in the first seconds and rarely revise it, so the advertiser's name tends to be heard and seen near the start (sooner on CTV, where people sit through less before deciding). It works best when no brand inside the story competes with it and the end card is the brand's own moment. [script-critique-before-production](wm://card/script-critique-before-production) on the script catches a weak or late brand before any picture costs money.
- **The brand beside the good state.** Viewers tend to remember a brand with the feeling it appeared next to, so the promoted brand usually stays away from the problem, the bad before or anything negative (its name on the "before" calendar, sign or frown). This is advertising practice, not something measured here. Making the problem general ("checkups used to be a chore") and bringing the brand in with the relief or the result does it.
- **The product is the real product.** Real references, a written spec of what it is in positive words, and its stated size anchored to a person at the same depth keep a model from inventing a nearby object. A clean packshot plate gives every shot the same one (hard rule 8; [object-and-known-thing-plate](wm://card/object-and-known-thing-plate)).
- **The same people, products and places in every shot.** A plate for anything seen twice, attached to every still that shows it (a person inside an ad or on a screen included), keeps identity from drifting ([location-plate](wm://card/location-plate)).
- **Sets look like a real place of that kind.** A family home has the rooms, furniture and clutter a family home has. A grand column or a showroom finish in a kitchen reads as wrong at a glance, even when nobody can say why. Naming the kind of place and its ordinary details in the plate prompt tends to work better than adjectives like "beautiful".
- **Physics holds, mirrors included.** A reflection shows what stands in front of the mirror, seen from the viewer's side. Someone looking into a mirror faces its glass, and the camera sees either their reflection or their back, never the mirror's back. Reflections, shadows and screens are where generated stills most often break this.
- **One moment stays put across its shots.** Between shots of one moment, people, props and wardrobe stay where they were. Shots of one place tend to hold together when they all come from one master still of that place, with close-ups cropped or edited from it rather than generated fresh, and a later state (lights back on, the wrapper opened) edited from the last still that showed that state. Shot-to-shot changes are cheapest to see on the stills side by side (`review` with `stills` in cut order): before animation they cost one still edit, after it a retake. Check the product beside its packshot at full size too (a still `review` with it in `products`).
- **Words that must read come from an image model, not the video model.** Video models garble lettering in motion, so a phone, TV or sign that has to be read tends to work as an image-model panel. Make it once and reuse it on every screen: cut to it full frame, or lay it on a dark screen with a `compose` `image` overlay. Screens in the background are safest showing nothing branded.
- **Takes and state changes** as defaults 1 and 3 describe.
- **Sound under every beat.** A bed or room tone under every shot keeps the spot from feeling empty. On TV, a deliberate silence works when it is short and carries room tone, and the hit after it is heard clearly.
- **The final is looked at, not only counted.** `review` the exact final with `requirements`, `characters` with plates and `expected_text` for every product and screen word, then open each visual observation's frame at full size. Fix what a viewer would notice at playback size: the defect count is not the goal. `ctv/standard` accepts only exactly 15 or 30 s at 1920x1080; deliver there for a TV brief, or to the destination the user names ([pitfalls-delivery.md](pitfalls-delivery.md)).

**Pitfalls**: invented imagery of the advertised property ([pitfalls-identity.md](pitfalls-identity.md)), product names and signs garbled in motion ([pitfalls-motion.md](pitfalls-motion.md) "Lettering in generated motion"; declare them in `review` `expected_text`), lip sync ([pitfalls-speech.md](pitfalls-speech.md)), narration timing ([pitfalls-narration.md](pitfalls-narration.md)), site pixels, delivery size ([pitfalls-delivery.md](pitfalls-delivery.md)).

**Questions to ask of the cut.**
- What will a viewer remember, and whose ad was it?
- Is the brand anywhere near the problem, or only with the good state?
- Do the sets, mirrors and reflections look physically real, and does each moment stay put across its shots?
- Does the first second earn the second?
- Is the proof visible, and is every claim sourced?
- Is the VO a rendered read in a chosen voice, with picture cut to its measured length?
- Under VO, are visible mouths still? Are logo, URL, price and legal lines exact?
- Is the CTA clear and last, with no dead tail, and loudness right for the destination?
- Does the end card belong to this film and its platform (end-card-design): carried from the film's world, words exact and still once they land, cut to the sound?
- Does the score change where the picture changes, and was it checked by `listen` with `picture: true` on a render of the draft with it (or, when a supplied track leads, does the cut follow it)?
- Is the delivery resolution what was asked (the destination's, else 1920x1080 for 16:9), from native 1088p or upscaled takes?
